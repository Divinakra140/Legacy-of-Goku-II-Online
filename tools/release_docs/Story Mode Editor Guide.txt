STORY MODE EDITOR GUIDE
=======================

There's a full story-mode creator built right into the game's own DEV TOOLS map editor - it's how the
built-in "Example - A Story Of Your Own: The Missing Key Capsule" mod was made, and it's open to anyone. You don't need to touch
the GBA cartridge or write any code: a story is just a list of steps you build in the EVENTS tab, which
you can watch run instantly with TEST IN THE GAME.

Open DEV TOOLS from the main menu: OPTIONS - DEV TOOLS (Windows and Mac).


1. STARTING A STORY
--------------------
Go to the STORY tab and give it a TITLE. From then on the main menu shows your story at the top, with
3 save slots, PLAY THE STORY and ERASE SLOT - it keeps its own saves, separate from normal heroes
(where you stood, flags, numbers, companions, opened places, who was shown or sent away).

Fields:
  TITLE          shown in the main menu and at THE END
  SUBTITLE       a line under the title
  HERO           who you play as
  START_MAP, X, Y  where a new game starts (use X, Y FROM THE MAP VIEW in the EVENTS tab to fill these
                 from wherever the MAP tab is currently showing)
  INTRO_EVENT    an EVENTS id that plays once, automatically, the moment a new game begins
  CREDITS        the lines that scroll at THE END
  CREDITS_MUSIC  the song that plays over them


2. THE EVENTS TAB: WHAT A STORY IS MADE OF
-------------------------------------------
An event is a little script: WHEN it starts (talking to someone, walking into an EVENT box, beating a
foe kind, the world starting, or - for your intro - nothing at all, it just plays), ONLY IF some
condition holds (flags, level, a mission done, an item), and ONCE or EVERY TIME. Then the steps
themselves, one under the other, run top to bottom unless something sends them elsewhere.

TEST IN THE GAME plays the event you're editing immediately, right where you are - this is the fast
feedback loop that makes building a story practical. Use it constantly: write two or three steps, test,
adjust, test again.

Steps, grouped by what they're for:

  BASIC       say, choice (its answers jump to labels), label, goto, if_flag, flag, give, take, zeni,
              exp, heal, quest (hand out a mission), finish_quest, wait, fade, warp (to a map and a
              spot), spawn (a foe), shop, sound, music, toast, end

  SCENES      move (someone, or PLAYER, walks to x, y; waits for it to finish, or not), face, pose (any
              pose the sprite set has - e.g. 18 is charging a beam, 9 is the transform animation),
              camera (to a spot or a person, with a zoom and a frame count), camera_back, shake,
              show_person, hide_person, emote (!, ?, ... over someone's head), and say / choice with a
              FACE (a sprite set shown to the left of the text box)

  NUMBERS     var (set, add, sub, mul, random), if_var (=, !=, <, <=, >, >=), timer (counts down,
              shown on screen), if_timer (has it run out?)

  STORY-ONLY  companion (a hero or sprite set fights beside you; leave to dismiss them), switch_hero
              (play as someone else - everyone keeps their own level), open_area / close_area (a box of
              the map becomes walkable or a wall - a boulder smashed, a bridge built; this is kept after
              saving), save (a save point, right here), the_end (rolls your story's own CREDITS)

  FORMS       unlock_form (give a transformation: from now on it is on the TRANSFORM button),
              lock_form (take it away again), transform (transform right now, into any form, unlocked
              or not - a story moment), revert (back to normal)

  BOSS FIGHTS boss_fight (kind, level, name, hp multiplier, where; a WIN label, a LOSE label, and
              phases like 0.5:half or 0.2:last - at that much health left, the fight pauses, jumps to
              that label, says its lines, and a fight_on step resumes it). The doors stay shut until
              it ends.

"%s" in a line of text becomes the hero's own name; "{var:coins}" becomes a number you're tracking.
A line too long for one box is flagged - SPLIT INTO BOXES THAT FIT cuts it up for you.
LINES FROM THE GAME searches every line any of the game's own people say - click one and it drops into
your step already carrying the right speaker and face.


3. HOW THE GAME'S OWN STORY FEELS, STEP BY STEP
-------------------------------------------------
This is the pattern the original game's cutscenes use - copy it freely:

  1. A cutscene: SHOW PERSON, CAMERA to them (zoom 1.5), EMOTE !, MOVE them to you, SAY with a FACE,
     CAMERA BACK.
  2. Asking around: PERSON-tool people each with an EVENT that adds 1 to a number (VAR add), then an
     IF VAR >= 3 that sets a flag once you've spoken to enough of them.
  3. A place you can only enter later: an EVENT box set to ONLY WHEN flags are set, a locked DOOR that
     needs an item, or a boulder (an EVENT box set to ONLY BY AN ATTACK, paired with OPEN AREA once
     it's smashed).
  4. A boss: BOSS FIGHT with a WIN label, a LOSE label, and a phase (say, 0.5:angry) - a SAY and a
     TRANSFORM step at half health, then FIGHT ON to continue the fight.
  5. SAVE, and right at the very end, THE END.


4. PLACING A PERSON OR AN NPC-ONLY LOOK FOR A SCENE
------------------------------------------------------
SHOW_PERSON drops someone into the map at a spot you choose, with their own sprite set, even if the
real map data has nobody standing there - useful for a character who should only appear for this one
scene. HIDE_PERSON removes them again by name.

If you've made a colour variant or a new look just for a cutscene (see COLORS - NEW VARIANT in the main
DEV TOOLS guide), you can swap a placed person onto it mid-scene the same way the port's own "FUTURE
GOHAN (CUTSCENE)" look does it: give them that variant's sprite-set number in SHOW_PERSON (or edit it
onto them afterwards), and pose/move them as usual - the editor doesn't need a separate step for this,
it's just which sprite set they're carrying at the time.


5. TESTING AND FIXING PROBLEMS
---------------------------------
- TEST IN THE GAME: plays the event you're editing immediately.
- F8 in the running game: the EVENT DEBUGGER. Shows the running event, its current step, and every
  flag, number, timer and state you're tracking. Invaluable for "why isn't this working" - it tells you
  exactly where the script actually is.
- F7 (with F8 open): jumps over the step the event is currently waiting on, so you can skip ahead
  without playing through everything again.
- CHECK EVERYTHING (the CHECK tab): looks over your whole story and flags what won't work - events
  that name people who aren't there, goto's aimed at a label that doesn't exist, boss phases written
  wrong, or a story intro that isn't a valid event id. Fix what it lists before you call it done.


6. A WORKING EXAMPLE
------------------------
The game ships with "Example - A Story Of Your Own" (modpacks, switched off by default). Switch it on
in MODS, then open its events in the EVENTS tab and read through them - it's a complete short story
(about 5 minutes: THE MISSING KEY CAPSULE) that uses nearly everything in this guide: a cutscene,
clues, a timer, a dark park, a two-phase boss, a locked door, a companion, and credits at the end.
It's the fastest way to see all of this working together rather than imagining it from a list.


GOOD TO KNOW
------------
- The story camera's zoom is built for one player; with two players on one PC it only moves, it
  doesn't zoom.
- Companions don't get hurt.
- The original GBA game's own story scenes can't be read out of the cartridge - they're compiled code,
  not data - so there's no shortcut to importing them automatically. But every line any of its people
  say IS readable, and shows up searchable under LINES FROM THE GAME, which is a real head start if
  you're rebuilding a scene from the original game by hand.
- Nothing you build here needs any ROM file to run or to share: SHARE - MAKE THE MOD FILE packages only
  what you made.
